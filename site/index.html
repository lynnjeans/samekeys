For everyone who moved from Windows to Mac

New Mac. Same Keys.

Your Windows shortcuts, on your Mac.

SameKeys makes Ctrl+C copy, Win+V open your clipboard history, and your mouse wheel scroll the Windows way, so years of muscle memory keep working.

Download for Mac View the source on GitHub

Free and open source · macOS 14 or later

One app, not five.

On a Mac, getting your Windows habits back usually means a separate app for each: one for the keyboard, one for the mouse, one for clipboard history… Each to find, install, allow and set up. SameKeys brings them all together, set up the Windows way from the first launch. That is why I made it.

Keyboard shortcuts

Ctrl+C, Home and End, F2: 59 Windows shortcuts, ready at first launch.

Mouse and wheel

Side buttons go back and forward, fn or Ctrl + scroll zooms the page, and the wheel turns the Windows way.

Clipboard history

Win+V opens what you copied, with search and pinning.

Window snapping

Finder

Delete, F2, cut and paste files, and New Document in the right-click menu.

Thought through for the switch.

Any keyboard

Works with a PC keyboard and with a Mac keyboard. Press Ctrl as on Windows, or use fn in its place and keep Ctrl for the Mac. Your choice, or both.

Terminals left alone

In Terminal and iTerm, Ctrl+C still stops the running program. SameKeys knows where Ctrl means something else.

Yours to adjust

Switch whole groups on and off, record a different shortcut for any entry, or write your own rules, for every app or just one.

Mouse and trackpad apart

Wheel direction and zoom change for the mouse only. Trackpad gestures keep working the Mac way.

Small and quiet

Lives in the menu bar and pauses with one click. No system extension or keyboard driver to install.

In your language

Clipboard history, like Win+V.

Press ⌥V for everything you copied recently, right where your pointer is. Pick one and it’s pasted.

  • Text, images and files, searchable
  • Pin what you paste often
  • Passwords from password managers are never kept
  • Stays on your Mac, never uploaded. Off until you turn it on.

Finder, the File Explorer way.

The keys and menus you used in File Explorer work in Finder and in every open and save dialog.

Explorer keys

Delete, F2 and Enter delete, rename and open. Backspace goes up a folder, and Ctrl+Shift+N makes a new one.

Cut and paste files

Ctrl+X, then Ctrl+V in another folder, moves the files there.

New in the right-click menu

Create a text, Word, Excel or PowerPoint document, copy a path, or open Terminal right there.

Paste a path

In Finder or a dialog, press Ctrl+Shift+G and paste a path to go to a folder or open a file, like File Explorer’s address bar.

Open and save dialogs

Ctrl+G jumps to the folder open in Finder, and Ctrl+Shift+G lists recent and favorite folders.

Favorite folders

Add a folder to SameKeys’s favorites from Finder’s right-click menu, and reach it from any dialog.

Two permissions, and why.

macOS asks you to allow two things before any app may change what your keys do.

Accessibility

Lets SameKeys turn a Windows shortcut into its Mac equivalent: you press Ctrl+C, the app receives ⌘C. Without it, macOS lets no app rewrite input.

Input Monitoring

Lets SameKeys see ordinary keys, such as the C in Ctrl+C. Without it, only modifier keys, clicks and scrolling arrive.

What you type stays yours. SameKeys never records, saves or sends it.

SameKeys records no keystrokes. It looks at each key press only to decide whether it’s a shortcut to translate, then lets it go. There are no analytics, and every line of the code is public.

Questions

How do I make Ctrl+C copy on a Mac?

macOS uses ⌘ (Command) for copy, paste and most shortcuts. Install SameKeys and it’s on: Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V, Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+S and the rest do what they did on Windows, everywhere except terminals, where Ctrl+C has to stop programs.

Why don’t Home and End work on a Mac?

On a Mac, Home and End scroll to the top and bottom of a document, and the start or end of a line is ⌘← and ⌘→. SameKeys sends those for you, and Shift+Home, Shift+End, Ctrl+Home and Ctrl+End select and jump as they did on Windows.

Can my mouse scroll the other way from my trackpad?

Not with macOS alone: its “Natural scrolling” setting turns the mouse wheel and the trackpad around together. In SameKeys, switch on “Windows scroll direction” on the Mouse page: the wheel then scrolls as on Windows, and the trackpad and a Magic Mouse keep natural scrolling.

Does it work with an Apple keyboard?

Yes. The Control key sits where it did on your PC. If you’d rather keep Ctrl for Mac shortcuts, choose fn and press fn+C to copy instead.

Is SameKeys free?

Yes, and open source under the GNU General Public License v3. Anyone can read, build and improve the code. If it saves you some frustration, you can sponsor the project, but you never have to.

How is it different from Karabiner-Elements?

Karabiner-Elements is a powerful tool for remapping single keys and building layers. SameKeys does one job, Windows habits on a Mac, ready to use at first launch, and handles the mouse and scroll wheel too. It needs no system driver.

Does SameKeys connect to the internet?

Only to check for updates, once a day. It reads the list of versions on this website and sends nothing about you or your Mac, and a new version installs only when you choose to. Turn off “Check for updates automatically” on the About page and SameKeys never goes online.

How do I cut and paste files on a Mac?

Finder has no Cut for files: you copy with ⌘C, then move with ⌥⌘V. With SameKeys, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V move files as they did on Windows, and the right-click menu can create new documents and open Terminal in the folder.

Can I snap windows to half the screen like Win+←?

Yes. With SameKeys, ⌥+← and ⌥+→ put the front window on the left or right half of the screen, ⌥+↑ fills the screen and ⌥+↓ minimizes. On a Mac keyboard, ⌥ sits where the Windows key is. macOS 15 and later also have fn+Ctrl+arrows built in.
